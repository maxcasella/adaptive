Running · Where fitness comes from

Easy miles build fitness.

At conversation pace, your muscles add mitochondria, the parts of a cell that turn oxygen into energy, and capillaries to bring the oxygen in. Both grow with time on your feet.

  • MitochondriaMore of them, so muscle uses oxygen faster
  • CapillariesMore tiny blood vessels around each fiber
  • Stroke volumeYour heart pumps more blood with each beat

Running · Lungs first, legs later

Lungs adapt before tendons.

Your heart and lungs improve within weeks. Tendons and bone take months. You'll feel ready for more miles before your legs are.

Running · The common mistake

Every run at the same pace.

Medium effort feels productive. It's too hard to recover by tomorrow and too easy to make you faster, so a week of it leaves you tired with little to show for it.

Running · Race day

Race day on fresh legs.

In the last two weeks your miles drop by about half and the race-pace work stays, so fatigue clears. Goal pace is one you've already held.
